# Home Utilities Dashboard — Prototype 1 Scope

**Project:** BRJ Labs · Home Utilities Software (Prototype-1)
**Status:** Draft for alpha-tester review
**Last updated:** 26 Sep 2026 (accounts, multiple households, remote access)

---

## 1. The problem

Shared-household utility costs are opaque and manually managed. Bills arrive scattered across inboxes and portals, someone has to do the math on who owes what, and nobody can see spending trends or plan ahead. Existing tools (spreadsheets, Splitwise, banking apps) all require manual data entry — that's the part that fails in practice.

**The idea:** a dashboard that _automatically_ ingests the household's utility bills, totals them monthly/annually across services, splits costs between housemates, and projects what's coming — all visible at a glance on any phone and on a shared wall display.

## 2. Prototype 1 goals

The first working version, alpha-tested by our own household, then used by family/friends' households on the same install. All households live on one server (the owner's NAS), reachable over the internet, each with its own admin, members and data.

A successful Prototype 1 means:

1. Bills for internet, electricity, gas, and water flow into the system with **zero manual typing** for supported providers (manual entry as fallback).
2. Any housemate can open the dashboard and see: current month's total, their personal share, what's due and when, and cost trends per service.
3. The system calculates each member's share and **tracks who has paid** (it does not move money — see §6).
4. Simple projections: "based on recent bills, set aside ~$X each this month."
5. A full-screen glanceable **wall view** running on an iPad in kiosk mode.
6. A non-technical person (the benchmark: the owner's aunt) can sign up, create or join a household, and get bills flowing **without configuring anything technical**. No mail rules, passwords to mailboxes, or settings files.

### Explicitly deferred (not in Prototype 1)

- Native iOS app (the web app is responsive; App Store comes after alpha feedback)
- Automated money pooling / direct debit of housemates (see §6)
- Hardware integrations (per-person water-flow sensing, smart meters)
- Consumer Data Right (CDR) energy/banking API integration
- Hosted SaaS as a business: billing, plans, a public marketing site, self-serve operations. Prototype 1 hosts several households on one self-hosted install, run by the owner (see §5 Accounts & households).

## 4. How it works — the pipeline

```
Provider email ──▶ Household's own bills address (e.g. smith-7k2f@bills.brjlabs.com)
                        │  Cloudflare Email Routing → Email Worker → app (signed request)
                        │  (also: PDF upload in the app; manual entry)
                        ▼
                  Ingestion service (routes by address → household)
                        │  saves raw email/PDF to NAS (audit trail)
                        ▼
                  Extractor (pluggable)
                   ├─ 1. AI reader (Anthropic API → what the document is + structured JSON;
                   │     text only, or the file itself for scans and photos)
                   └─ 2. Low confidence or no AI? → Manual-review queue in the UI
                        ▼
                  Bill record in database
                        ▼
        Dashboard · Splits · Projections · Wall view
```

Key properties:

- **Zero setup for households:** each household gets a unique bills address on the owner's domain. Members forward each bill to it from their own inbox as it arrives (owner decision 2026-09-28, after asking a non-technical tester: forwarding fits day-to-day life; nobody changes the email on their provider accounts). No mailbox credentials, no forwarding rules. **Upload a bill** takes a PDF directly.
- The single-mailbox IMAP poller built for our household was removed on 2026-09-29: the per-household address is the only email path.

- **Raw bills are kept** on the NAS (`inbox/` folder, filed by year, month and service) — audit trail and re-parse source; deleting a bill deletes its file. From a forwarded email only its PDF attachments are kept; an email with no PDF is kept as the bill itself.
- **LLM extraction** (Anthropic API) turns any bill layout into `{provider, service_type, amount, billing_period, due_date, usage + units}` plus every other figure on the bill (usage by tariff, readings, charges, rates, averages, comparisons) with one prompt — no per-provider parser maintenance. Volume is ~10–30 calls/month (cents).
- The extractor is an **interface**, not a hard dependency: the AI reader reads every document (no per-provider templates ship; the slot for one remains), manual queue last. Swapping or adding extraction methods later (local LLM, CDR APIs) touches config, not architecture.
- Anything unparseable becomes a "needs review" card with a pre-filled manual-entry form — the system degrades gracefully, never silently misses a bill.

## 5. Core features

### Dashboard (phone-friendly web)

- This month's total across all services + per-service breakdown
- **Your share** front and centre for the logged-in member
- Upcoming bills with due dates
- Trend charts per service (monthly, with same-period-last-year comparison as data accumulates)
- Annual running total and cost-per-service ranking

### Housemate splitting

- Configurable split ratios per service (default: equal)
- Per-member ledger: what you owe this cycle, what you've paid, running balance
- "Mark as paid" + payment reference generation from the bill payer's stored payment details (PayID/BSB, IBAN, Venmo, etc. — configurable per member)
- Reminder nudges for unpaid shares (in the app and by email)

### Personal services and dashboards (owner, 2026-09-29)

- A member can keep their own services (car rego, car insurance…) in the household's install: a **personal** service is seen only by that member (admins included), isn't split, and its bills stay out of every household figure (totals, charts, projections, the wall, area comparisons, other members' lists and emails).
- Car rego, car insurance, roadside assistance, gym and vehicle repairs that a member uploads or forwards are guessed to be theirs (a per-type setting in the catalog); anything else is guessed to be the household's, and whoever brought the bill in is asked "Who's this for?" once. Any service can be switched between household and personal from its bill or its page: its owner hands it back; a household one can be made someone's own by whoever set it up or brought every one of its bills in, and by an admin or its payer only when nobody is known to have. Refused once a housemate has paid a share of one of its bills.
- Home has a **Household · Mine** switch in households with more than one member. "Mine" shows what the member pays, all in: their share of household bills plus their personal bills, kept apart — this month, this financial year, a 12-month trend, where the money goes, what they owe, and their personal bills and services.

### Sub-services (owner, 2026-09-30)

- Subscriptions and Donations are main services that hold sub-services: each streaming service, app or charity (Netflix, Spotify, Red Cross) is its own service with its own bills, split and owner, listed under its main service in the nav (behind an arrow), on the Services page and on the main service's own page. A new provider's bill sets one up automatically; one can also be added by hand.
- A sub-service can be cancelled: it records who cancelled it and when, keeps every bill, and a bill that arrives dated after the cancellation is flagged ("charged after cancelling").
- Any bill can be moved to another service or sub-service from its page.
- Any service can be recurring (on by default for subscriptions and donations): the next charge is shown as upcoming at the latest price; once its date passes with no bill in it's recorded as an assumed bill, and the real bill, when it arrives, takes its place. A price change on the real bill carries forward and the household is told.
- Every service type has one colour, the same in every household; households don't pick service colours.

### Vehicles, Health and Lifestyle (owner, 2026-09-30; reworked 2026-10-01)

- **Vehicles**, **Health** and **Lifestyle** are main services whose sub-services are their types: Vehicles → Registration, Car insurance, Roadside assistance, Repairs & servicing; Health → Health insurance, Ambulance cover; Lifestyle → Gym, Donations (a service per charity) and other lifestyle types. **Subscriptions** is its own main service. All sit in the nav (behind an arrow), on the Services page, in the Bills filters and in the Move list.
- Vehicles, Health and Lifestyle read the same way: a tile each first (a vehicle, or a service: its last 12 months, about how much a month, what's next), each opening its own page; then all of them together (the four figures, where the money goes, the year ahead, each month). "Add a vehicle" / "Add a service" is the button top right, opening its own page.
- **A vehicle's page** is laid out like a service's (the year ahead and each month by kind — Rego, Insurance, Roadside, Servicing — every bill by year, its figures, where its money goes, who each kind is with). **A service's page** (Ambulance cover, the gym, Netflix) is the usual service page: every bill, its cost over time, its figures, how it's split. In the nav, Vehicles opens to the vehicles, the others to their services.
- **Subscriptions** is a plain list like the Bills page: a row per subscription (its price a month, its next charge, where its latest stands; the row opens its page), cancelled ones after; beside it all subscriptions together a month (and a year), with a coloured line of which take the most.
- A vehicle is entered once (a name, an optional number plate or VIN, the household's or one person's own) on its own page; bills not filed under a vehicle wait in a "Not filed under a vehicle" card on Vehicles, each with "Which vehicle?". Health and Lifestyle have no vehicles or policies.
- A new bill that prints a vehicle's plate or VIN is filed under it by itself; adding a vehicle, or giving it a plate, files the waiting bills that print it.
- A bill is on its vehicle's side: a household vehicle's bills are the household's and split as usual; someone's own vehicle's bills are theirs alone. Filing a bill under a vehicle on the other side moves it there (same rules as moving a bill: only whoever brought it in can take it out of the household, and not once a housemate has paid a share). Changing whose a vehicle is moves its bills with it.
- A bill can be moved to a sub-service and a vehicle in one step from its page (a rego notice filed under insurance → Registration · Ford Fiesta), and the bill form has a Vehicle field.

### The Services page and the Bills filters (owner, 2026-10-01)

- The Services page shows one month at a time (‹ October 2026 ›, up to next month): what's due, paid, to pay and still to come that month, then a line per service with what it has due that month (by due date; each instalment in its own month), where it stands and the change from the bill before, or when its next bill is expected. Main services fold their services away, closed by default.
- A service whose bills follow the calendar at a (nearly) fixed price (internet, a subscription) shows its price over time (a step line, a dot per bill) instead of cost bars of one height.
- The Bills page has one filter bar: search, All · To pay · Paid · Unusual, and a Filters button (service, year, whose, which months) whose choices show as removable pills.

### Projections

- Next-bill estimate per service from recent cycles, seasonally aware (winter gas ≠ summer gas)
- "Set aside ~$X each this fortnight/month" recommendation per member

### Wall view (`/wall`)

- Full-screen, glanceable, auto-refreshing route designed for an iPad in kiosk mode
- Household total, who-owes-what status, next due bill, trend sparkline
- Design target: readable from across the room, no interaction needed

### Manual review & entry

- Queue for failed/low-confidence parses
- Plain manual-entry form (covers paper bills and portal-only providers)
- Upload a bill (PDF) straight into the extraction pipeline

### Accounts & households

- **Sign up** with Google or with an email address + one-time code (no passwords to forget). After signing up, a new account chooses: **Create a household** (becomes its admin and runs the setup wizard) or **Join a household** (paste a join code, or use the invite link from their email).
- **Invitations:** a household admin adds members by email. The app emails the invite and also shows a copyable link/code to send by text. Invites expire and work once.
- **Members before they join:** an invited member already exists in the household (their shares are calculated from the day they're added), so splitting never waits on someone accepting.
- **Personal PIN:** after accepting, each member sets their own PIN. It's a quick unlock on devices where they've already signed in properly; only they can change it. The admin can remove a member but can never see or set their PIN or sign in as them.
- **Roles:** _member_ (their own shares, mark paid, view household), _household admin_ (services, bills, members, settings for that household), _operator_ (the owner of the install: software updates, system status, usage caps across households). A household only ever sees its own data.
- **AI extraction cap** per household per month, so an open sign-up can't run up the operator's API bill.

## 6. Money: calculate and track, never hold

Prototype 1 **never touches funds**. Holding or moving other people's money is a regulated activity everywhere (AFSL in Australia, EMI/PSD2 in the EU/UK, MSB licensing in the US), so settlement is **calculate and track only**:

- **What the app does:** works out each member's share of a bill, gives it a payment reference, and tracks it as paid or unpaid when the household marks it. Whether the bill itself was paid to the provider comes from receipts, payment notices and auto-pay.
- **What it never does:** collect, hold, pool or send money. Members pay each other and the provider through their own banks.
- **Later:** a licensed pooling or direct-debit service (shared-account products, bill-splitting services) could be added as its own module that talks to that provider. The licensed provider holds the money, never this app.

## 7. Architecture & deployment

| Decision      | Choice                                                                                                       | Why                                                                                                                                |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| App           | **Next.js (TypeScript)**, full-stack, single repo                                                            | One language everywhere; fast to build and iterate with Claude Code; React ecosystem for the iOS-feel UI                           |
| UI            | Tailwind CSS + Recharts                                                                                      | Apple-like clean design; charting for trends/projections                                                                           |
| Database      | **SQLite** (single file) via ORM; every household-owned row carries `household_id`                           | A handful of households fits comfortably in one file; the DB lives on a ZFS dataset, so **ZFS snapshots = the backup strategy**    |
| Ingestion     | Per-household address via Cloudflare Email Routing + Email Worker → app; PDF upload                          | No mail server on a residential IP, no mailbox credentials per household                                                           |
| Outbound mail | A transactional sending service on the owner's domain (e.g. Resend)                                          | Invites and sign-in codes                                                                                                          |
| Extraction    | Anthropic API (pluggable extractor interface)                                                                | One prompt handles all providers in any country; also classifies service type and normalises units (kWh, MJ, therms, m³, kL, gal…) |
| Deployment    | **Single Docker container**, built by GitHub Actions and published to GHCR; TrueNAS Scale Custom App         | One image, one port, one volume; Settings → Software update pulls the new image (NAS cron job, snapshot first)                     |
| Data location | TrueNAS ixVolume mounted at `/data` → `db/` (SQLite), `config/`, `inbox/` (raw bills)                        | Stored with the NAS's other app data                                                                                               |
| Access        | Internet via **Cloudflare Tunnel** on a brjlabs.com subdomain; LAN `http://<nas-ip>:30080` for the wall iPad | Other households must reach it from anywhere; the tunnel needs no open ports on the router                                         |
| Auth          | Accounts: Google OAuth or email one-time code; per-member PIN as quick unlock on signed-in devices           | Internet-facing, so a PIN alone is never enough from a new device                                                                  |

External dependencies: **Cloudflare (tunnel, email routing), a sending service for invites and codes, Google OAuth, the Anthropic API key, and GitHub (code + image registry).**

## 7a. International-ready by design

The alpha household is in Australia, but the alpha build is the version most likely to be shown to investors and to households in other countries. Nothing country-specific lives in core logic:

- **Household settings** (one record): currency (ISO 4217), locale (BCP 47), IANA timezone, fiscal-year start month, week start. Every total, period boundary, and display reads from these.
- **Money** is stored as integer minor units plus a currency code; display uses `Intl.NumberFormat`. Tax (GST/VAT/sales tax) is an optional amount + label on a bill.
- **Dates** are stored as ISO-8601; extractors receive a locale hint so DD/MM vs MM/DD is resolved per provider.
- **Units** go through a registry (kWh, MJ, therm, m³, kL, gal, ccf, GB…) with canonical conversions. Service types (internet, electricity, gas, water, rent, custom) and billing cadences (weekly … annual, irregular) are configurable data.
- **Payment details** per member are generic label/value pairs (PayID, BSB/account, IBAN, Venmo, Zelle…), and payment references come from a template.
- **UI copy** is externalised (next-intl) from day one. English only in the alpha; other languages are a data change.
- **Demo mode** boots a fictional household so the product can be shown without exposing real bills.
- Australian defaults (AUD, en-AU, July financial year) live in `.env.example` and seed data only.

## 8. Data model (sketch)

- **Household** — one per household: name, currency, locale, timezone, fiscal-year start, week start, bills address, AI usage this month
- **Account** — a person who can sign in: email, display name, Google ID (optional); operators are the accounts named in `OPERATOR_EMAILS`
- **Member** — a person in a household's splits: name, role (admin/member), linked account (empty until the invite is accepted), PIN hash (set by the member), active date range (move-in/out pro-rating), payment details as ordered label/value pairs
- **Invite** — household × email → token (hashed), expiry, accepted_at; plus a per-household join code
- **Trusted device / session** — account × device, created by a full sign-in; unlocked by PIN afterwards
- **Service** — type (internet/electricity/gas/water/rent/custom), provider, billing cadence, default split
- **Bill** — service, amount (minor units) + currency, optional tax amount/label, billing period, due date, usage + normalised unit, source (template/llm/manual) + confidence, link to raw document, status, anomaly flag
- **Bill metric** — every other figure on a bill: stable key (e.g. `usage_peak`, `supply_charge`), label as printed, kind (usage / reading / charge / rate / average / comparison / other), value + unit or amount (minor units) + currency, optional sub-period. Groundwork for usage limits and period comparisons across all services
- **Share** — bill × member → amount owed, paid_at, method
- **Projection** — service × month → estimated amount (computed, cached)

## 9. Privacy & security notes

- All bill data stays on the owner's NAS; only extracted bill _text_ is sent to the LLM API for parsing, except scans and photos (no text layer), which are sent as the file.
- No personal inbox access, ever: bills arrive at an address the app owns.
- **Internet-facing:** reachable through a Cloudflare Tunnel (no router ports opened). HTTPS-only cookies, rate-limited sign-in and PIN attempts, single-use expiring invites, no member names shown before sign-in. A Cloudflare Access policy can sit in front as an extra gate.
- **Household isolation:** every query is scoped to the signed-in member's household; one household can never read another's data.
- **Personal services:** within a household, a member's personal services, their bills and the documents behind them are shown only to that member; other members get "not found" even with a direct link.
- **Operator visibility:** the owner of the install can technically see every household's data. Alpha testers are told this up front.
- The app never holds credentials that control the NAS (updates go through a request file and a host cron job).

## 10. Alpha success criteria

After ~2 billing cycles with our household:

- [ ] ≥80% of bills ingested with no manual typing
- [ ] Every member checks their share without asking anyone
- [ ] Zero missed/lost bills (review queue catches all failures)
- [ ] Projection within ~15% of the actual next bill
- [ ] Wall iPad is genuinely glanced at (the honest test)
- [ ] At least one outside household says "I want this" after a demo
- [ ] An outside household (the aunt test) signs up, creates or joins a household and gets a bill in without help beyond the invite

## 11. Roadmap after Prototype 1

1. **Alpha feedback round** — family/friends' households, structured feedback
2. **Licensed settlement** — integrate a licensed pooling / auto-collection service as its own module (the app still never holds funds)
3. **Native iOS app** (SwiftUI) + push notifications; wall-display product support as the market develops
4. **CDR integration** — regulated energy + banking data APIs to supplement or replace email ingestion
5. **Hosted offering** — billing, plans, self-serve operations on top of the multi-household core (multiple households on one install is now in Prototype 1)
6. **Hardware experiments** — per-person usage attribution (e.g. shower flow sensing)
7. **Tariff and plan tracking** — store rate plans; "you'd save $X on plan Y" comparisons (switching referrals are the obvious monetisation path)
8. **Carbon estimate** — regional emissions factors over normalised kWh/MJ usage
9. **Notifications beyond in-app** — web push, email digest, Telegram/Slack webhooks
10. **Read-only guest links** — landlord, accountant, or a departing housemate
11. **Community template parsers** — declarative rules so other households add providers without a release
12. **Open Banking / CDR payment confirmation** — close the "did they actually pay" loop automatically
13. **Product metrics** — % of bills auto-parsed, time saved, money tracked; logged from the alpha onward for the pitch deck
14. **Seasons, weather and regional insights** — season-aware flags and forecasts, household postcode, weather-adjusted usage, and opt-in pooled area figures. Full scope (approved and built, S1–S4): [SCOPE-seasons-weather-regions.md](SCOPE-seasons-weather-regions.md)
